A passport, a visa, and permission to enter a country serve different purposes. Confusing them can lead to expensive travel mistakes—particularly when someone assumes that a valid U.S. visa guarantees entry or that its expiration date determines how long they can stay. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

Here is a general explanation of the distinctions, followed by questions to check before making travel plans.

## A passport is a travel and identity document

A passport is issued by a country to document the holder's identity and nationality for international travel. It is not, by itself, permission to enter every other country.

Destination and transit requirements can depend on your nationality, itinerary, and document validity. Check the relevant government's requirements rather than assuming that a passport accepted on a previous trip will meet the rules for the next one. U.S. passport holders can start with the State Department's destination information. <sup><a href="#source-3" aria-label="Source 3">3</a></sup>

## A U.S. visa allows you to seek admission

For travelers who require one, a U.S. visa generally allows travel to a U.S. port of entry to request admission in the relevant category. It does not guarantee admission. U.S. Customs and Border Protection makes the admission decision at the port of entry. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup>

Different visa categories permit different purposes of travel. A visitor visa is not a general authorization to work in the United States. If your plans change, do not assume that the same document permits the new activity. <sup><a href="#source-1" aria-label="Source 1">1</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-4" aria-label="Source 4">4</a></sup>

## Visa validity and permitted stay are different

The expiration date on a visa generally concerns when it can be used to seek admission, subject to its terms. It does not necessarily establish the length of an authorized stay after admission. <sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup>

For many admitted nonimmigrants, the Form I-94 arrival/departure record provides the admission classification and an admitted-until date or notation. These records can require careful interpretation. An unfamiliar notation, an apparent error, or uncertainty about the end of an authorized stay should be addressed through official channels and, where needed, qualified legal advice. <sup><a href="#source-2" aria-label="Source 2">2</a></sup><sup><a href="#source-5" aria-label="Source 5">5</a></sup>

## Check these questions before booking

1. **What is the purpose of the trip?** Tourism, a business visit, study, and employment involve different rules.
2. **Which documents are required?** Check both the destination and any transit countries.
3. **Are the documents valid for the planned itinerary?** Review passport, visa, and other entry-document requirements.
4. **Do you need an appointment or a new application?** Use the relevant embassy or consulate's current instructions.
5. **Could travel affect an immigration matter?** A pending application or a question about status may require advice before departure.

Avoid relying on an old blog post for current fees, interview availability, or entry restrictions. Government requirements and appointment conditions can change.
